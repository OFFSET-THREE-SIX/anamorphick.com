# Dominion Cover Art — Production Brief

Two covers, one visual family, shot practically (no generative imagery).

- **Dominion Defense** — the operator. Heroic, backlit, single figure, arctic.
- **Dominion Dynamics** — the system. Cold, wide, high-key, human small or absent.

The split is the point: Defense is a *person*, Dynamics is a *place and a machine*.
Same grade family, opposite energy. They should hang next to each other and read as
one company with two temperaments.

---

## 0. Legal line — read first

We are borrowing **compositional grammar**, not artwork.

| Not protectable — use freely | Protectable — do not touch |
|---|---|
| Low camera angle, upward tilt | The Frazetta *Conan the Barbarian* (1966) painting, in whole or recognizable part |
| Pyramid/triangular figure mass | That painting's specific combination: planted axe + mound of bodies + clinging figure at the leg |
| Backlit silhouette against a luminous sky | The name "Conan" (Conan Properties International trademark) |
| Desaturated earth palette + one accent hue | AT-AT, snowspeeder, tauntaun, Rebel insignia, Echo Base interiors (Lucasfilm) |
| Ice-planet look: white field, blue shadows, wind-driven spindrift, twin low suns | The word "Hoth" anywhere client-facing |

Frazetta Properties LLC actively enforces. A photograph that reproduces the pose,
the mound, the axe and the clinging figure together is a derivative work regardless
of it being a photo rather than a painting. **Change the specifics, keep the mechanics.**

Also strip the reference names from delivered file names, layer names, and any deck
that leaves the building. If Defense sells into DoD, their marketing review will flag
anything that reads as a licensed franchise faster than any lawyer will.

## 1. What actually makes that cover work

Decomposed, so we can rebuild it from parts:

1. **Low camera, upward tilt.** Camera at knee height, tilted up 8–12°. Makes the
   subject monumental and pushes the horizon into the bottom quarter.
2. **Pyramid mass.** Figure is the apex; a dark, abstract base mass spreads below.
   The base is *not readable* — it's a value shape, not a subject.
3. **One dominant value contrast.** Mid-dark figure against a luminous field, with
   the brightest point of the whole frame directly behind the head and shoulders.
   That halo is doing 70% of the work.
4. **Negative space above the head.** ~20% headroom. Nothing in it. That's what
   reads as "mythic" — and it's where the wordmark goes.
5. **A vertical anchor, planted down.** A long object the figure rests weight on.
   Planted = dominance and aftermath, not action. Never raised overhead.
6. **A small secondary element, low in frame.** Gives scale and a second beat of story.
7. **Three hard edges only.** Face, the anchor object's business end, and the forward
   knee. Everything else soft. This is edge control, not focus — do it with light
   and selective post, not aperture.
8. **One accent hue.** Everything else desaturated.

That's the whole formula. It is not owned by anyone.

## 2. Dominion Defense — the arctic cover

### Concept
Single operator, low horizon, standing on a broken sea-ice pressure ridge, backlit by
a low arctic sun through haze. Dark figure, luminous white-blue field. We **invert**
the original's value scheme — dark-on-light rather than dark-on-smoke — and the arctic
gives us the halo for free.

### The anchor object — DECISION NEEDED
The planted vertical is the single element that tells the viewer what Defense does.
Pick one:

- **A.** Sensor/antenna mast driven into the ice — reads *domain awareness, watching*.
  Best if Defense is C4ISR, radar, or Arctic surveillance.
- **B.** A hardened case or pelican stack with a deployed dish — reads *expeditionary,
  we set up where you can't*.
- **C.** A rifle, muzzle-down, or a ski pole — reads *operator, presence*. Most literal
  transfer of the original, and the most generic.

A is the strongest and the least legally adjacent. Confirm before the shoot — the prop
drives the whole silhouette.

### Camera
- 35mm (full-frame equivalent). Wider distorts the figure; longer kills the monumentality.
- Camera height 24–30". Tilt up 8–12°.
- f/5.6–f/8 so the ridge holds its shape. Depth is not the separator here; value is.
- Subject apex at ~18–20% from the top. Horizon in the bottom third.
- Portrait, 1:1.5. Crop for 1:1 and 16:9 derivatives from the same frame — frame loose.

### Lighting
The whole cover is one hard backlight through haze.

- **Key (the "sun"):** one hard source — bare bulb or 7" reflector with a 20° grid.
  Placed behind subject, 15° off the lens axis, raised to ~25° elevation. This is your
  rim and your halo in one.
- **Atmosphere:** haze the air between key and camera. This is non-negotiable — the
  bloom around the figure *is* the effect. Hazer, not a fog machine; you want even
  suspension, not rolling clouds.
- **Fill:** 8×8 white bounce flat on the floor in front of the subject. On location,
  snow does this for you — it's the one gift the arctic gives.
- **Face:** 4×4 silver just off camera axis at **−2.5 stops** under key. Just enough
  to hold the eyes. Any more and you lose the silhouette.
- **Background:** if in studio, 20×20 white seamless lit to **+1.5 stops** over key at
  the halo point, falling to key level at the frame edges. That gradient behind the
  head is the "sky."

### Grade
- Split tone: shadows toward cyan (~200°), highlights toward amber (~40°), both at
  low saturation. Blacks crushed but not clipped — hold 4–6 IRE of detail in the ridge.
- Exactly one accent hue in frame. A sodium marker light, a hazard stripe, a single
  amber lens element. Nothing else warm.
- Selective clarity: raise on face / anchor tip / forward knee. Drop everywhere else.

## 3. Dominion Dynamics — the ice-planet cover

### Concept
Opposite register. No hero. Wide, flat, overcast, near-white. A machine or structure
at scale, human figures reduced to specks for measure, atmospheric perspective
dissolving the background to nothing. The feeling is **systemic, engineered, cold** —
not brave.

### Camera
- 50–85mm equivalent, or wider for a true landscape plate. Camera at eye height —
  no heroic tilt, that belongs to Defense.
- Horizon dead center or high. Let the white field dominate.
- Landscape, 16:9 primary. This one is a banner, not a book cover.

### Lighting
- **No key.** Overcast/softbox sky. On location, shoot under flat cloud; in studio,
  a large overhead soft source with no directional accent.
- Shadows filled entirely by ground bounce. Shadow-to-key ratio under 1.5 stops.
- Atmospheric perspective is the subject: haze the deep background until the far plane
  goes to near-paper-white. Layer it — foreground crisp, midground soft, background gone.

### Grade
- No split tone. Uniform blue-gray across the curve.
- Lift shadows 12–15%, flatten the contrast curve, desaturate to 15–25%.
- Highlights slightly cyan. Never let anything go warm — a single warm pixel breaks it.

## 4. Practical snow (studio option)

- **Ground / drifts:** coarse salt or Epsom over foam-board contour. Sodium
  polyacrylate ("instant snow") for the top dressing — it reads wet and crystalline
  under hard backlight the way salt does not.
- **Pressure ridge:** carved rigid foam, skimmed with joint compound, dusted with
  polyacrylate. Build it as a *value shape*, not a texture study — it lives in shadow.
- **Falling / spindrift:** paper snow through a fan at low volume. Shoot bursts;
  composite the best pass.
- **Breath:** doesn't fake convincingly. Either shoot cold on location or omit it.
  A bad breath plate is worse than none.

## 5. Three ways to shoot it

| | Approach | Cost | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Location, one day, real snow, magic hour | Lowest | Weather, talent exposure, single-take pressure |
| **B** | Full studio composite — figure on black, snow plates separate | Highest | Comp integrity; backlight haze must match across plates |
| **C** | **Hybrid: figure in studio under the correct light, comped onto an owned/licensed arctic plate** | Middle | Lowest — recommended |

Go **C**. Shoot the figure in a controlled hard backlight with the haze in-camera so
the rim and bloom are real, then place it on a plate. The thing that never comps
convincingly is light wrapping through atmosphere — so make that part real and let
the landscape be the borrowed element.

---

## 6. Layout

Leave **22% headroom** above the figure on Defense for the wordmark — type sits in the
top band over the sky, the same place a paperback cover puts it. Dynamics takes the
wordmark lower-left over the flat white field, where there's nothing competing.

Both covers: no type below the horizon line.

---

## 7. Deliverables

- Defense: 1:1.5 hero, plus 1:1 and 16:9 crops from the same capture.
- Dynamics: 16:9 hero, plus 1:1 crop.
- Ungraded TIFF masters retained; graded 16-bit for print, sRGB JPEG for web.
- One shared LUT/grade recipe applied to both so the family reads.
